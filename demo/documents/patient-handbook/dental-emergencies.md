# Dental Emergencies

Maple Grove Dental Studio keeps time every day for urgent cases. Call (212) 555-0147 as early as possible and we will see you the same day whenever we can.

If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, swelling that is spreading to your eye or neck, a high fever with facial swelling, or an injury to the head or jaw, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. These situations can be life-threatening and need a hospital, not a dental office.

## What counts as a dental emergency

- Severe toothache that does not let you sleep or does not improve.
- Swelling of the face or gums.
- A tooth that was knocked out or pushed out of place.
- Bleeding from the mouth that does not stop after 20 minutes of pressure.
- A broken tooth with sharp edges or pain.

A lost filling, a chipped tooth without pain, or a loose crown are urgent but usually not emergencies. Call us during business hours to book a toothache or emergency exam.

## Knocked-out tooth

Time matters: a permanent tooth has the best chance of being saved if it is put back within one hour.

1. Pick the tooth up by the crown (the white part), never by the root.
2. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly with milk or water. Do not scrub it or wrap it in a tissue.
3. If you can, gently put it back in its socket and bite on a clean cloth to hold it.
4. If that is not possible, keep it in a cup of milk or inside your cheek, and come to the clinic immediately.

Baby teeth should not be put back in place. Bring the child to the clinic to check the area.

## Broken or chipped tooth

Rinse your mouth with warm water and keep any pieces of the tooth in a clean container. Hold a cold pack on the cheek to reduce swelling. Avoid chewing on that side until you are seen.

## Swelling or abscess

A painful swelling on the gum, sometimes with a bad taste, may be an abscess, an infection that needs treatment. Rinse with warm salt water and call the clinic the same day. Do not put heat on the swelling and do not try to drain it yourself.

## Cost of an emergency visit

A toothache or emergency exam includes the exam and an X-ray of the painful area and starts at $79 without insurance. The dentist then explains the treatment options and their cost before doing anything else.
