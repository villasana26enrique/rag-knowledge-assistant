# Sterilization and Infection Control Protocol

Every team member who handles instruments or prepares treatment rooms must follow this protocol. It is based on standard infection control practices for dental offices. When in doubt, ask the clinical lead before continuing.

## Personal protective equipment

Wear gloves, a mask, protective eyewear and a clinic gown for every procedure. Change gloves between patients and whenever they tear. Wash or sanitize your hands before putting on gloves and right after taking them off.

## Preparing the treatment room

Between patients, clean and disinfect every surface touched during the procedure: the chair, light handles, the instrument tray and the countertop, using the approved disinfectant wipes and leaving the surface wet for the contact time on the label. Replace barrier covers on the light handles, the chair controls and the X-ray sensor.

## Instrument processing

Instruments are processed in the sterilization room in this order:

1. Transport used instruments in a closed, labeled container.
2. Clean them in the ultrasonic cleaner for 10 minutes. Never clean sharp instruments by hand.
3. Rinse, dry and inspect each instrument for remaining debris.
4. Package instruments in sealed pouches with a chemical indicator, and write the date and the autoclave cycle number on each pouch.
5. Sterilize in the autoclave at 134 °C for at least 4 minutes, or at 121 °C for at least 30 minutes.
6. Let the pouches dry completely before storing them in closed cabinets.

Pouches are opened in front of the patient, right before the procedure.

## Monitoring the autoclave

- Check the chemical indicator on every pouch after each cycle. If it did not change color, the load must be processed again.
- Run a biological (spore) test once a week, every Monday, and record the result in the sterilization log.
- If a spore test fails, stop using the autoclave, tell the clinical lead immediately and recall the instruments processed since the last passing test.

## Sharps and waste

Dispose of needles and blades in the red sharps container right after use; never recap a needle by hand. Replace the container when it is three-quarters full. Contaminated waste goes in the red bag; everything else is regular trash.

## Exposure incidents

If you are stuck by a used needle or splashed in the eyes or mouth, wash the area immediately (water for the eyes and mouth, soap and water for the skin), tell the clinical lead, and fill in an incident report the same day. The clinic covers the cost of the medical follow-up.
